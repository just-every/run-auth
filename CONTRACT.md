# Auth target product contract

Target scope approved 16 October 2026. Auth is a full workforce identity and single sign-on provider for a US employer replacing **Okta Workforce Identity, Microsoft Entra ID or JumpCloud**: one company directory, passkey-first MFA, SAML/OIDC into the company’s SaaS and legacy apps, HR-driven provisioning/deprovisioning and governed access. It remains the one sign-in for the owner’s RER apps, an owner-run MIT product declaring `oidc-provider@1` without depending on that interface itself. USD, US employers and US employment/timezone norms are the first commercial context.

The buyer is the US IT/security owner who must get every employee and contractor into the right apps on day one, change access as their job changes, and prove it ended when they leave. Rippling IT (identity), authentik and Zitadel are the comparison set for modern adoption; the push-further clauses add bounded agents that finish and verify that work. This target is the product being built toward, not a description of today’s release. It changes no approved homepage, interface or runtime behavior.

The owner-approved workforce scope supersedes the earlier CIAM positioning in category research. Its useful table-stakes—SDKs, hosted/embedded flows, organizations, profile APIs, enterprise connections, token lifecycle and migration—are retained here as workforce/app interoperability, not used to turn Auth into a consumer application platform. No neighboring unowned category is absorbed.

## How to read this contract

Each named clause is a buyer promise with a public-door acceptance journey, repeated with status in ACCEPTANCE.md. The final status table is grounded in code-audit commit `cdd103e1ceeeedbacf52badf7a51bfc6b18296f6`, also the main revision read for this edition; no implementation changed since that audit. The input audit covers 64 inventory rows (10 done, 23 partial, 31 missing); clause counts below differ because clauses organize the whole workforce job. `done` means the bounded clause has implementation and cited automated/journey evidence, not hosted acceptance or certification. `partial` names delivered parts and absent target behavior. `missing` has no implementing product path. Target journeys for unfinished behavior are requirements, not reports of a test run.

Interactive administration by the administrator roles AUTH-DECISIONS-001 lists (owner, identity administrator, security/policy administrator, app administrator, help desk, auditor with export rights and every custom administrator role) retains the existing hardware passkey and fresh local email requirements. Everyday business decisions—a manager, app owner or group owner approving or denying access, a reviewer certifying access, an employee requesting it—are not administration: they take an ordinary employee passkey sign-in plus the step-up policy requires (AUTH-DECISIONS-001), and may be made from Slack or Microsoft Teams (AUTH-CHAT-001) except grants of privileged roles. Machine administration, such as Terraform or a nightly cleanup script, uses scoped admin API credentials published by a human under AUTH-ADMINAPI-001 and AUTH-TERRAFORM-001; it never impersonates interactive proof, and preparer keys (AUTH-ADMIN-001) remain propose-only. Existing RER client registrations retain AUTH-PROTOCOL-001 unchanged. Broader interoperability is a separate explicit profile under AUTH-OIDC-001 and AUTH-TOKENS-001.

## Retained identity and RER guarantees

- **AUTH-HOST-001 — Issuer host.** The host owns the issuer and passkeys. Choose it before linking apps. A host change is a provider move requiring the apps' owners to approve re-linking; it does not silently preserve an issuer or passkey origin. Fresh local setup selects localhost; explicit and saved hosts are preserved.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Choose localhost on fresh setup; repeat with an explicit/saved issuer; enroll a passkey and link an app. Attempt a host move and require a new app-owner approval rather than preserving issuer authority.

- **AUTH-PRIVACY-001 — Shared claims.** Auth shares each person's email and name with the apps they use. Under RER Managed, RER can sign link approvals for the account's Auth install; it receives people's data only as an ordinary linked app, for people who sign in to RER through it.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Sign in to two linked apps with different granted identity scopes; inspect UserInfo and tokens for only the permitted claims. Add a RER approval key and confirm it grants no user-data access without an ordinary app sign-in.

- **AUTH-RER-TRUST-001 — RER trust.** Auth accepts a RER link approval only when an active trusted key signed it (`typ: rer-link-approval+jwt`, `aud` this issuer, lifetime at most five minutes, unique `jti` consumed once), its origin and link key match the app document Auth pulls itself, and its `claim`, if any, names the Auth owner. Keys come from the `RER_LINK_APPROVAL_KEYS` setting of a RER-provisioned install, or from an owner or administrator on Sign-in methods with passkey and fresh local email proof. Withdrawal, by an administrator there or by RER with an assertion signed by that key when the account leaves Managed, ends the key for good. RER-made links are flagged and notified like token-made links. An install with no trusted key makes no request to RER.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Link using a correctly signed RER approval, then refuse wrong issuer/origin/key/claim, stale assertion and jti replay. Withdraw trust through both supported doors and prove the key cannot be restored or used again.

- **AUTH-IDENTITY-001 — Owner and sign-in proof.** Base bootstrap claims the owner but conveys no Auth privilege. Email-link proof completes once, only in the requesting browser. First passkey enrollment requires Auth email proof; administration and additional passkey enrollment require hardware passkey proof. Every enrollment, recovery approval or completion and administration write also requires this browser's Auth email confirmation within ten minutes, rechecked with the session's authority in the committing write; an enrollment challenge is valid only in the session that requested it. Recovery of anyone holding an administrator or other privileged role (AUTH-DECISIONS-001) requires another administrator's approval, a cancelable notified 72-hour delay, or the local emergency recovery token, and this rule is never relaxed for them. A non-privileged employee may also take the same-business-day help-desk path of AUTH-RECOVERY-003; today every person follows the administrator rule, which is stricter than the target and satisfies it.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Through the real email, WebAuthn and administration routes, prove same-browser one-use confirmation, first/additional enrollment, fresh local email step-up rechecked at commit, wrong-session and revoked-session refusal, and approved/delayed/canceled/emergency recovery.

- **AUTH-PROTOCOL-001 — OpenID Connect profile.** Auth implements code/PKCE with state, nonce, RFC 9207 issuer, private_key_jwt clients, pairwise subjects and ID tokens accompanied by signed bearer access tokens. Access tokens are restricted to Auth UserInfo, bound to client, subject, session and granted scopes, and valid for at most five minutes or the remaining session lifetime. UserInfo supports GET and POST, returns only scoped identity claims and rechecks active session, account, client, grant, email proof and domain policy. ID tokens and other bearer credentials cannot authorize UserInfo. No refresh tokens or offline access are offered for the static code-flow profiles. Existing clients retain ES256; clients may select RS256. Both algorithms’ keys are encrypted under AUTH_ENCRYPTION_KEY, published before use and retained through outstanding token expiry. These guarantees are exercised by the edition tests; they are not a certification statement.

  **Acceptance evidence:** A registered RER client completes code/PKCE/private_key_jwt, validates nonce/issuer/audience/pairwise subject, and calls scoped GET/POST UserInfo. Refuse code/assertion replay and inactive client/person/session/grant/policy; test encrypted ES256/RS256 publish-before-use and retirement.

- **AUTH-BOUNDARY-001 — Private authorization.** Every private document and API enforces its own session and administrative policy. The seed denies inherited Base operator, invitation, provider and domain management paths. Only bootstrap, Base session inspection and local Base sign-out remain exposed; gated dev entry exists exclusively in the development bundle. Removing the gate removes dev-session authority; production refuses dev proof.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Request each private page/API signed out, as an email-only member, as a passkey administrator and with revoked authority; deny inherited Base management and production dev tokens. Remove the local dev gate and confirm authority ends.

- **AUTH-MOVEIN-001 — Staged move-in.** Directory exports are staged apart from people: a staged row cannot sign in, receives no mail and appears only on the move-in review. Admission creates the same invited member as a People invitation, with no email proof, administrator right, password, factor, app role, group permission or session, and sends mail only when the owner chooses to send invitations. A matching email never rebinds an existing person, passkey or upstream link. Undo removes only people the batch admitted who have never signed in and hold no passkey, app grant or later activity, and records who it removed and kept. An Auth0 former subject is data for app owners: no protocol endpoint accepts it and it never replaces the pairwise subject. Staging, admission, invitation and undo are administration writes under AUTH-IDENTITY-001.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Stage Google, Entra, Auth0, Okta and JumpCloud exports through Move-in, inspect collision/disabled rows, admit selected members without proof/roles/mail, explicitly invite, and undo only untouched admissions. Refuse former Auth0 subjects as credentials and concurrent/stale administrative authority.

- **AUTH-ADMIN-001 — Agent preparation.** An agent prepares administration and a person decides. A preparer key, minted, renewed and revoked by an administrator under AUTH-IDENTITY-001, is shown once, stored as a digest, expires 30 days after minting or renewal, and is accepted only by the preparer API while unrevoked and while its accountable administrator remains the administrator who minted it: it ends for good when that person is demoted, suspended or removed, and promoting or restoring them again does not bring it back (a new key is minted). It reads an allow-listed inventory (no session, passkey, key or token material) and files proposals to suspend or restore a person, invite a person, remove an invitation, or add or remove a domain's required provider; filing stores the affected people and apps and a digest of the target's state, and changes nothing else. A proposal runs only on one exact approval: an administrator's passkey signature over a challenge naming it, with that session's fresh local email proof, rechecked with the approver's authority, the unchanged target state and an unexpired, undecided proposal in the committing write. It runs once; it never suspends the owner or its own approver; its outcome is recorded in Activity with the key and the approver. A preparer key confers no session, page, approval, key management or protocol access and is not an access token.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Mint/renew/revoke a preparer key, inspect its allow-listed inventory, file each supported proposal and approve its exact state with a person’s passkey and fresh local email. Prove expiry, stale-state refusal, one winner under concurrent approvals and permanent loss of key authority after administrator removal.

- **CMD-001 — Commands.** Auth retains the shared RER command dispatcher and verbs. Its edition-owned extensions are documented in AUTH-COMMANDS.md. Setup applies real D1 migrations and claims the first owner. Sign-in begins in the browser, never by presenting an unusable CLI-bound proof. Local recovery writes only a token digest to D1 and stores the raw token in a mode-0600 private file.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Run real install/setup/dev, start browser sign-in, repeat setup without replacing owner/settings/data, stop the managed process, and generate/redeem local recovery with only a digest in D1 and a mode-0600 raw token file.

- **HTTP-001 — HTTP surfaces.** Hono serves health/API dispatch and Start serves remaining documents; Auth intercepts identity, protocol and private Auth pages before dispatch. Unknown routes return 404.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Call health, Auth APIs and public/private documents through the emitted Worker; confirm identity interception, per-request authorization and an ordinary 404 for unknown routes.

- **AUTH-LINK-001 — App links.** Auth links apps through Base's pull protocol (LINKING.md). It reads only the app's own documents at its exact origin and binds the exact callback set. Codes are one-use, last 60 seconds and are redeemed atomically. It verifies the redeem assertion (audience, at most 60 seconds, unique `jti`). A client keeps its id and sector. A new key needs an administrator who saw the existing link, and a link replaces another app (its sector at another origin, or another sector at its origin) only on an approval that named it. Link tokens cover one origin and callback set, are single use and last at most an hour. Production installs refuse loopback and plain-http origins; dev installs serve only loopback apps. Token- and RER-made links are flagged and announced. Reported interface versions are kept, and a lower one, reported or re-linked, waits for the owner. Removing an app signs out everyone it signed in.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Use the pull/link-token flow to link a real Base app Worker, sign in, re-link with continuity, and remove it. Refuse mismatched callbacks/origins, replay/concurrent redemption, unauthorized key swap and unapproved downgrade; observe linked-app sessions end on removal.

- **AUTH-LOGOUT-001 — Sign-out reach.** Sign out everywhere, suspension (identity fence) and RP-initiated logout send signed `logout+jwt` tokens with a unique `jti` and no `nonce` to each linked app's registered logout path. Each is retried for up to 24 hours. Discovery advertises `end_session_endpoint` and back-channel logout with session support.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Sign out everywhere, suspend a person and request RP logout through real Auth/app endpoints; the app rejects the old session and replayed logout tokens. Exercise bounded retries and inspect the persisted delivery outcome; discovery advertises the supported endpoints.

- **AUTH-TRANSFER-001 — Export and import.** Export carries people, emails, passkey public keys, clients, grants, subjects, history and a signed grant map. It omits sessions, private keys, the pairwise key and the RER keys an owner trusted; withdrawn RER keys stay withdrawn. Import refuses app origins the destination cannot serve and active dev personas. A restore at the same host keeps every client, sector and pairwise subject.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Export a populated installation, import into an empty installation at the same host, and re-link the app with unchanged client/sector/pairwise subject. Inspect retained records and absent authority; refuse incompatible origins and active dev personas.

## Target clauses

### The workforce directory and employee experience

- **AUTH-DIRECTORY-001 — One workforce directory.** Employers manage employees, contractors, service identities, managers, departments, locations, employment dates, immutable person/source identifiers, custom attributes and active/invited/suspended/terminated states. Search, filter, bulk preview and admin API edits (made through AUTH-ADMINAPI-001 credentials or an administrator's session) use the same validation and audit. Static and attribute-driven groups have explainable membership, group owners and mapped app roles; source precedence and manual overrides are visible. Rehires and email/name changes preserve reviewed identity continuity rather than merging by email.

  **Acceptance evidence:** In People/API, onboard a W-2 employee and a contractor, rename and transfer one, preview a bulk edit, evaluate dynamic groups, suspend and rehire; verify stable identifiers, explicit source conflicts and resulting assignments without duplicate accounts.

- **AUTH-TENANCY-001 — Organizations and delegated administration.** An installation can serve multiple employer organizations, subsidiaries and partner workspaces. Installation authority and organization authority are separate: the installation owner and installation administrators run the installation, and each organization has its own owner. Each organization is either installation-operated, where installation roles also administer it, or isolated, where installation roles have no read, write or export authority over its directory, profiles, audit content, issuer secrets or connector credentials and the installation owner can only host it or erase or reset it as a whole under AUTH-ERASURE-001. People may hold distinct memberships and roles in more than one organization, with an explicit active organization. Owner, identity administrator, security/policy administrator, app administrator, group owner, manager, help desk, auditor and employee permissions are scoped and server-enforced, including exports and API credentials; each built-in role's permissions are listed, and employers compose further roles and scopes under AUTH-ROLES-001 (custom roles, resource sets of groups/apps/people, separate view and edit scope, rank fence on administration). Invitations expire, can be canceled, and never grant more than the inviter can delegate. Employers can delegate setup without exposing another employer's directory or issuer secrets.

  **Current scoped administration semantics (ct 21 Oct 22:55Z; public-door conformance proven):** The original installation retains its issuer and tables. New employers have separate physical stores, path issuers, factors, sessions and signing keys; installation roles confer no directory read/write/export authority in an isolated organization. Existing HTTP tests cover proven-session switching, expiring/cancelled invitations, one-app/one-case delegation and cross-employer denials. All current organizations are isolated. Scoped credential custody and the 1440/390 authority journey pass; named HTTP assertions now cover installation-owner/admin read/write/export and issuer/connector credential refusals, including renamed cookies. The current authority journey passes at 1440 and 390. Complete organization-schema restoration remains under AUTH-TRANSFER-002; current archives fail closed without provisioned destination schemas.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Invite a contractor into two employers, switch organization, delegate one app to an app administrator and one recovery case to help desk; deny cross-organization reads/writes/exports and unauthorized role escalation, including direct API requests.

  **Current isolation semantics:** every newly created employer is isolated. Installation roles acquire no employer session, membership or directory authority by hosting it. Installation archive export/import refuses an active isolated employer, including an active employer inside a source archive; anonymous evidence from an erased employer remains portable, with credentials and sealed pending remote work excluded. AUTH-TRANSFER-002 inherits a separate employer-owner-authorized archive/restore door before that capability ships. A future installation-operated mode inherits the published permission/resource/rank and independent-proof boundaries before it ships.

- **AUTH-TENANCY-002 — No cross-organization linking or proof transfer.** A person's memberships in different organizations are never linked, merged or matched automatically, whether by email, domain, upstream subject, device, imported metadata or an agent suggestion. Proof, factors, recovery state, sessions, consent, approvals and administrator authority completed in one organization never satisfy another; each organization's sign-in, enrollment and step-up stand alone. A person's own organization switcher requires their proof in each organization and gives no organization visibility of another's records. Records retained after an organization is erased or reset (AUTH-ERASURE-001 anonymous receipts and sealed remote work) are never attached to, shown in, matched against or retried from any other organization, including a new organization later created with the same name, domain or people. The only link between a person's identities is one they create under AUTH-ERASURE-002; it carries no proof, visibility or authority.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Give one person memberships in two isolated organizations with the same email and upstream subject; confirm no automatic link or match, that a passkey sign-in, step-up, recovery or approval completed in one does not satisfy the other, and that an administrator of one cannot see or act on the other membership through UI, admin API or export. Reset one organization, create a new organization with the same name, domain and people, and confirm none of the retained receipts or sealed work appears in, links to or can be retried from it.

- **AUTH-ROLES-001 — Custom administrator roles and resource sets.** Employers build custom administrator roles from a published permission list (for example: view people, edit profile fields, suspend/restore, reset factors, approve recovery, manage group membership, manage group rules, configure app, assign app, manage policy, manage identity providers, view audit, export audit, manage admin API credentials). A role is granted over a resource set: named groups, attribute-defined groups (such as "non-privileged users in the US group"), apps and individual people, with view scope and edit scope set separately. The built-in AUTH-TENANCY-001 roles are expressible in the same model. Server enforcement applies to UI, admin API and exports alike, in this order: organization boundary, published permission, view or edit resource set, then rank. Built-in administrator ranks are immutable: installation owner and organization owner 100 (each in its own scope), installation administrator 90, identity administrator and security/policy administrator 80, app administrator 50, help desk 20, auditor 10. Group owner, manager, app owner and employee carry administrator rank 0; their decisions follow AUTH-DECISIONS-001. A custom role's rank (1–89) is chosen by the installation owner, an installation administrator for an installation-operated organization, or that organization's owner, always strictly below the chooser's rank. Rank grants nothing by itself. No role can administer (profile, status, factors, recovery approval, roles, sessions or that person's data export) a person whose administrator rank in that scope is equal to or higher than its own, nor anyone outside its edit scope. Viewing follows view scope, not rank. A role holder can never grant a role, rank, resource set or admin API credential wider than their own current authority. Admin API credential custody is the published "manage admin API credentials" permission; until custom roles ship it is held by installation owner and installation administrator. Role and resource-set changes are administrator writes under AUTH-IDENTITY-001, previewed with who gains and loses which actions, and audited. Future machine administration doors inherit AUTH-ADMINAPI-001 credential, scope/resource, excluded-action, live-authority, audit and concurrency checks before shipping.

  **Current scoped administration semantics (ct 21 Oct 22:55Z; public-door conformance proven):** Custom and built-in roles, separate view/edit resource sets, membership-sensitive decisions, previewed grants and audited scoped commands are implemented. Existing HTTP assertions cover equal-rank account-administration refusals, higher-owner administration, viewing without a rank fence and refusal of administrator factor reset. The ratified permission/resource/rank order and immutable built-in ranks are published in `docs/administrator-roles.md`. Named HTTP journeys prove scoped admin API credential custody, rank/no-widening, owner-only isolated custom-role design, exact gain/loss previews and hardware/fresh-email gates; the authority journey proves the current controls at 1440 and 390. Future machine doors inherit AUTH-ADMINAPI-001, and ordinary decisions inherit AUTH-DECISIONS-001 before shipping.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Create a help-desk role with "reset factors" over group A and view-only over group B. As its holder, reset a factor for a person in A; be refused editing in B (view succeeds), refused on an administrator in A, and refused via direct API and export. Attempt to grant the role over a wider set and be refused; change group A's membership and observe the next decision follow it.

- **AUTH-DOMAINS-001 — Verified employer domains.** Administrators prove control of a corporate domain through expiring DNS or equivalent ownership challenges before domain-based enrollment, discovery or federation routing is activated. A domain cannot be silently claimed by another employer. Preview shows who a rule affects, aliases are explicit, and removal/reverification cannot strand emergency owners. Verified domain control never by itself proves an individual’s identity or licenses account linking.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Claim a domain through the admin setup door, fail an incorrect or expired challenge and competing claim, then enroll and route an eligible worker. Revoke domain ownership and confirm policy reevaluation and audited recovery access.

- **AUTH-PROFILE-001 — Profiles and safe identity linking.** Employees can read and edit permitted profile fields through account UI/API; administrators manage typed custom metadata with field-level visibility and source ownership. Linking or unlinking Google, Microsoft, Okta, GitHub, Apple or generic upstream identities requires proof of the existing account and the new identity, explicit confirmation and a surviving sign-in/recovery path. An issuer/subject pair has one binding; matching email, imported metadata or an agent suggestion alone cannot take over an account.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Edit an employee profile and protected HR field, connect a second proven upstream and then unlink it; refuse an email collision, foreign subject, concurrent binding and last usable sign-in removal. Inspect app claims for only consented fields.

- **AUTH-ENROLLMENT-001 — Managed enrollment and password compatibility.** Invitation, approved-domain self-registration and HR-driven prehire flows share verification, duplicate resolution, terms/consent and recovery controls. Passkeys are the default. HR-sourced future hires activate before their start date through AUTH-ONBOARD-001, and anyone without a usable factor gets a first credential through a temporary access pass (AUTH-TAP-001). Employers migrating password-dependent workforces can explicitly enable hashed password sign-in, governed by AUTH-PASSWORD-001's per-group policy and reset or unlocked under AUTH-PASSWORD-002, and email or SMS one-time verification; these methods cannot confer hardware administration proof or bypass conditional access. Public registration stays closed unless employer policy opens it, and a pending invitation or import is never authenticated authority.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Enroll an invited employee and an approved-domain self-registrant, reject an outside address, complete a password reset under AUTH-PASSWORD-002 and a one-use SMS/email challenge, and prove weaker methods cannot administer or bypass required MFA. Cancel enrollment and verify its outstanding links stop.

- **AUTH-PASSWORD-001 — Password policy per group.** Wherever an employer enables password compatibility (AUTH-ENROLLMENT-001), the Auth password is load-bearing: it carries RADIUS/VPN and Wi-Fi EAP-TTLS (AUTH-LEGACY-001), LDAPS binds, Mac password sync (AUTH-DESKTOP-001), the WS-Trust endpoint (AUTH-M365-001) and AD write-back. It is therefore governed by versioned password policies assigned to groups in an explicit priority order, with a default policy for everyone else; each person's effective policy and why it applies are shown. Each policy sets: minimum length (8–64, default 12) with at least 64 characters always accepted; required character classes (lower case, upper case, digit, symbol, each optional); refusal of any part of the person's username, email local part, first or last name; refusal of Auth's list of at least 100,000 common and dictionary passwords plus employer-added words; breached-password screening by a k-anonymity range query (Have I Been Pwned Pwned Passwords) when a password is set and, by policy, at sign-in; history of the last 0–30 passwords (default 24); minimum age of 0 to 7 days (default 1 hour); maximum age of never or 1–999 days, with an expiry warning a set number of days before (default 14) shown at web sign-in, in the launcher, at the desktop login window and by email, and a forced change at the next sign-in once expired; and lockout after 1–100 consecutive failed attempts (default 10), with automatic unlock after a set period or never, self-service unlock under AUTH-PASSWORD-002 and an optional lockout notice (AUTH-NOTIFY-001). Failed attempts count across every door that accepts the password (web, RADIUS, LDAPS, WS-Trust, desktop). Lockout blocks only the password: passkey sign-in, push, help-desk recovery (AUTH-RECOVERY-003) and a temporary access pass (AUTH-TAP-001) still work, so a remote attacker cannot lock a person out of their account. Policies preview who they affect before publishing, follow group membership on the person's next password set or sign-in, and are administrator writes under AUTH-IDENTITY-001; every policy version is audited and included in the AUTH-REVIEWS-001 evidence export.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Assign a policy of length 12, history 24, minimum age 1 hour and lockout at 10 attempts to group A, and a policy of length 16 with symbols required and a 90-day maximum age with a 14-day warning to group B; preview the affected people. Through the web change door and a RADIUS sign-in, refuse for a person in A each of: 11 characters, a password from their history, a second change within the hour, a password containing their last name, a block-list word and a breached password; refuse a password without a symbol for B. Fail 10 attempts for A's person across web and RADIUS and see the password locked while passkey sign-in still succeeds; self-unlock under AUTH-PASSWORD-002. Age B's person's password into the warning window and past expiry, and see the warning at each surface and the forced change. Move the person from A to B and see B's rules at their next change.

- **AUTH-PASSWORD-002 — Self-service password reset, unlock and propagation.** Employees reset a forgotten password and unlock a locked password themselves at the hosted sign-in, in the launcher, in the Auth mobile app and at the macOS and Windows login window (AUTH-DESKTOP-001). Policy sets, per group, which factors reset and unlock require: by default one strong factor (passkey, Auth push or Auth TOTP), and, only for a person with no strong factor enrolled, email or SMS plus a second check (an SMS code together with an email link); security questions are never offered. Email or SMS alone is never accepted for anyone who has a stronger factor enrolled, nor for an administrator or privileged role (AUTH-DECISIONS-001), who reset only through a strong factor or AUTH-IDENTITY-001's recovery. Reset and unlock responses do not reveal whether an account exists and are throttled under AUTH-ABUSE-001. A reset or change is checked against the person's AUTH-PASSWORD-001 policy, ends their other password-based sessions as policy sets, sends the AUTH-NOTIFY-001 password-changed notice and propagates to every place the password is used: Auth verifies RADIUS, LDAPS binds and WS-Trust itself, so those take the new password at once; the AD connector writes it back to Active Directory (AUTH-LEGACY-001); and the Mac Platform SSO password-sync account takes it at the next online login or unlock. All of this happens within AUTH-TIMING-001's 60-second local bound, after which the old password is refused at every door Auth controls. A target that cannot yet confirm (connector offline, Mac not yet online) is shown per target as pending, never as done, and stays an AUTH-FOLLOWUP-001 obligation. Help desk can force a reset under AUTH-RECOVERY-003 with a temporary password that must be changed at first use.

  **Acceptance evidence:** For a person in group A with Auth push enrolled, reset a forgotten password through self-service with push, and be refused when offering email alone; unlock a locked password the same way. Within 60 seconds see the new password accepted and the old one refused at GlobalProtect RADIUS, an LDAPS bind, the WS-Trust endpoint and the Mac login in password-sync mode, and read the written-back password's change time from AD. Take the AD connector offline during a reset and see AD pending until it returns. Request a reset for an unknown address and receive the same response as for a known one; refuse a self-service reset by email alone for an administrator.

- **AUTH-ONBOARD-001 — New-hire activation before the start date.** For a future hire from an HR source (AUTH-HRIS-001, AUTH-HRIS-002) or created by an administrator, Auth sends an activation invitation to the personal email address on the HR record (or one an administrator enters, marked as an unverified personal contact) at an employer-set lead time before the start date (default 7 days, 0 to 30), with reminders through AUTH-FOLLOWUP-001 and a notice to the hiring manager if it is still unused 2 days before start. The activation link is single use, bound to the person and expires after a set period (default 7 days); reissuing it voids the old one. Opening it proves the personal address; employer policy may add a second check (an SMS code to the HR-recorded mobile number, or an ID-document and selfie check through Persona or CLEAR, whose result and not the document is stored). The hire then enrolls a passkey and the Auth authenticator (AUTH-AUTHENTICATOR-001), and a password where AUTH-ENROLLMENT-001 enables it. Enrollment grants nothing before the authorized start (AUTH-TIMING-001): launcher tiles read "available from" the start time, and every launch, token, SAML, RADIUS and desktop sign-in is refused until then. A hire who has not enrolled by the start uses a temporary access pass (AUTH-TAP-001). An HR start-date change moves the gate; a rescinded offer revokes the enrollment and its factors. The personal address is used only for activation and for recovery notices the employer enables, never as a work sign-in identifier.

  **Acceptance evidence:** From an HR test source, add a hire starting in 10 days with a 7-day lead time; see no invitation until 7 days before start, then the invitation at the HR personal address. Enroll a passkey and push before start; be refused app launch and token issuance until the start time, then launch. Let a second hire's link expire, reissue it and refuse the old link. Move a start date earlier and later and see the gate follow; rescind an offer and see the enrollment revoked. Confirm no message reaches the personal address after start except enabled recovery notices.

### Authentication, SSO and policy

- **AUTH-MFA-001 — Passkey-first multifactor policy.** Employers require phishing-resistant platform/synced passkeys or hardware security keys, offer TOTP and single-use recovery codes under explicit assurance rules, and control enrollment, replacement and factor loss. SMS/email are policy-limited bootstrap/recovery alternatives with lower assurance, never equivalent to a security key. The Auth mobile authenticator (AUTH-AUTHENTICATOR-001) adds push with number matching and its own TOTP; each factor's assurance (phishing-resistant, push, TOTP, SMS/email) is published and policy chooses which satisfy which app, RADIUS flow (AUTH-LEGACY-001) and OS login (AUTH-DESKTOP-001). Factor inventory, lost-factor revocation, step-up and the named employee security notifications of AUTH-NOTIFY-001 cover web, desktop and native mobile; a lost factor without a recovery code is restored the same business day under AUTH-RECOVERY-003. Existing hardware-passkey and fresh local email gates for interactive Auth administration by the roles AUTH-DECISIONS-001 lists remain mandatory.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Enroll a platform passkey, hardware key, Auth push and TOTP, require step-up for a sensitive app, revoke a lost factor and consume a recovery code once. Deny downgraded admin access and prove factor removal/rotation reaches current sessions according to policy.

- **AUTH-AUTHENTICATOR-001 — Push authenticator in the Auth mobile app.** The iOS and Android Auth app (AUTH-MOBILE-001) enrolls as an authenticator bound to a device key held in the Secure Enclave/StrongBox and protected by biometrics or device PIN. It delivers push approvals showing the requesting app, employee, approximate location, IP, device and time, with number matching (the employee types the number shown on the sign-in screen) wherever the requesting flow can display one; it also generates TOTP offline. Push carries no approval in the notification payload itself: approval is a signed response from the enrolled device key, single use and bound to the transaction, expiring after 120 seconds. Denying a push refuses the sign-in and optionally reports it as suspicious, which notifies the security owner and can suspend further pushes to that person (push-fatigue control: rate limit and automatic number matching after repeated denials). Phishing-resistant passkeys remain the default for browser sign-in; push and TOTP are the stated factors for RADIUS/VPN, Wi-Fi and other flows where a passkey cannot cross the protocol, and policy can require a passkey instead wherever one can. Lost-device removal invalidates the device key at once.

  **Acceptance evidence:** On real iOS and Android builds, enroll the authenticator, approve a web sign-in with number matching, deny another and see it refused and reported, and complete a sign-in with the app's offline TOTP. Replay an approval, answer after expiry and answer from a second unenrolled device; each is refused. Send repeated pushes and observe the fatigue control; remove the device and prove its next approval is refused.

- **AUTH-POLICY-001 — Conditional access and session policy.** Versioned policies combine employee/group/app/role, authentication assurance, managed-device posture, IP/network/location, the named behavior signals of AUTH-RISK-001 (new device, new country/state/city, geo-location radius, velocity, IP reputation) and session age; no condition is an unexplained score. Administrators simulate impact before publishing; an explainable decision records the matched rule, each signal's value and evidence freshness. Deny or require step-up when posture is missing/stale, enforce idle/absolute session lifetime and fresh authentication on critical actions, and reevaluate when membership, location, device or policy changes. Emergency accounts are explicitly bounded, monitored and drilled rather than a blanket bypass. Future machine administration doors inherit AUTH-ADMINAPI-001 credential, scope/resource, excluded-action, live-authority, audit and concurrency checks before shipping.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Preview and publish a payroll-app rule requiring a managed US device and fresh passkey; attempt allowed, foreign-network, stale-posture and expired-session launches through app and direct protocol routes. Add an AUTH-RISK-001 "step up on new country" condition and see a new-country sign-in stepped up with the signal named in the decision. Change group/policy during sign-in and verify the current decision at token issuance.

- **AUTH-ABUSE-001 — Automated attack protection.** Configurable bot challenges, suspicious-IP/device throttling, credential-stuffing and brute-force detection, breached-password screening, lockout and user/security-team notification protect every enrollment, sign-in, reset and token endpoint. Decisions are tenant-scoped and observable, avoid account enumeration and do not let a remote attacker permanently lock out emergency recovery. Risk-driven step-up uses AUTH-RISK-001's named signals, lockout and self-service unlock follow AUTH-PASSWORD-001 and AUTH-PASSWORD-002, and the user notification is AUTH-NOTIFY-001's; all retain the same account proof and administrator controls.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Attack real authentication/reset/token HTTP doors with bot traffic, password spraying, a breached password and distributed source attempts; observe rate limits/lockouts, protected notifications and legitimate recovery. Sign in through a Tor exit and from a location implying impossible travel, and verify step-up or denial with the AUTH-RISK-001 signal named in an explainable log.

- **AUTH-RISK-001 — Named sign-in behavior signals.** AUTH-POLICY-001 rules use named, tunable behavior signals, each computed from the person's own sign-in history: new device (a browser or device not seen within the person's last N successful sign-ins, by device cookie or bound device key; N defaults to 20); new country; new US state; new city; new geo-location beyond a set radius (default 20 km) from all of the last N locations; velocity (implied travel speed from the previous successful sign-in above a set threshold, default 805 km/h, about 500 mph); new IP address and new network (ASN); and IP reputation: anonymizing proxy or VPN service, hosting provider and Tor exit node from a named, dated IP-intelligence feed (MaxMind GeoIP2 with Anonymous IP by default), an employer block list and employer-trusted corporate networks. Recent failed attempts and denied pushes are also signals. Each signal is a boolean or a number in the rule language, so "step up on new country, allow new city, deny Tor" is a rule an employer writes directly. Data that is unavailable (for example a failed location lookup) has its own named value, and the rule states what it does then. Signals are evaluated at every sign-in and token issuance through every door: web, OIDC, SAML, WS-Federation, desktop, and RADIUS where the connector reports the client IP. A summary risk level may be displayed, but it is always broken down into the signals that produced it; no rule can depend on an opaque score. Each decision records the signal values and the feed versions it used, and AUTH-POLICY-001 simulation replays the last 30 days of sign-ins against a draft rule.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Publish "step up on new country, allow new city, deny Tor" for a group. Sign in from a known location and pass; from a new US city and pass with "new city" logged; from a new country and be stepped up; from a location implying 900 km/h travel since the previous sign-in and be stepped up with velocity logged; through a Tor exit and be denied. Change the radius and look-back and see the next decision follow them. Each decision log names the signal, its value and the feed version. Simulate the draft rule over 30 days of history and see per-signal counts.

- **AUTH-NOTIFY-001 — Employee security notifications and suspicious-activity reports.** Auth sends each employee named security notifications by email to the work address, and as a push in the Auth app where enrolled, for: sign-in from a new device or browser (AUTH-RISK-001's new-device signal), with app, time, approximate location, IP and device; factor enrolled; factor reset or removed, by them or by help desk; password changed or reset; password locked; recovery started (with its cancel action); temporary access pass issued; and an administrator role granted. Employers can turn off individual types except recovery started, factor reset or removed, and administrator role granted, which always send. Every notification carries a one-click "Report suspicious activity" action that works from the email without signing in (a signed, single-use link valid for 7 days) and from the Auth app. A report records a suspicious-activity event linked to the triggering event, alerts the security owner through AUTH-FOLLOWUP-001 (email, Slack or Teams, and Oncall per policy), and by policy ends all the person's sessions and refresh grants with back-channel logout and in connected SaaS apps (AUTH-LOGOUT-002), requires a password reset and factor review at next sign-in, or suspends the person pending help desk. Reports and their outcomes are audited, appear in AUTH-AUDIT-001 investigations and stream to the configured SIEM. A notification never contains a sign-in link or code that could itself confer authority.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Sign in from a new browser and receive the new-device email with its stated context. From the new-device email, while signed out, click "Report suspicious activity": see the security owner's escalation in Slack and, under the end-sessions policy, the person's sessions ended and a linked app's back-channel logout delivered. - **AUTH-LOGOUT-002 — Universal logout from connected SaaS for a compromised account.** When an account is suspected compromised but the person is not leaving, Auth ends their sessions inside connected SaaS apps as well as in Auth, without deprovisioning them. Triggers are: an administrator's or help desk's "End all sessions" action on a person within their AUTH-ROLES-001 scope; an AUTH-NOTIFY-001 suspicious-activity report under the end-sessions policy; an AUTH-POLICY-001 rule on AUTH-RISK-001 signals (for example a Tor-exit or impossible-travel sign-in after a successful one); and an approved AUTH-AGENT-002 or AUTH-AGENT-005 action. - **AUTH-SAML-001 — SAML 2.0 workforce identity provider.** Auth supplies SP-initiated and explicitly permitted IdP-initiated SAML SSO for company apps, with per-app metadata import/export, entity IDs, exact ACS allow-lists, NameID/attribute/group/role mappings (AUTH-MAPPING-001) and certificate rollover with overlapping publication. - **AUTH-MAPPING-001 — Attribute and NameID transform language.** Per-app SAML attributes, NameID and OIDC claims, SCIM/API attribute pushes and generated usernames are computed by a documented, versioned expression language over the person's directory, source and group data. - **AUTH-ERASURE-001 — Authorized erasure with retained accountability.** Owners erase under explicit retention/legal-hold policy, after an exact preview tied to the current revision and counts and a confirmation rechecked at commit, with hardware passkey and fresh local email proof (AUTH-IDENTITY-001); erasure is never scheduled and no erase permission is delegable. Unfinished remote work is reported separately and keeps only the minimum destination identifier it needs, sealed, until it completes or is explicitly abandoned. A legal hold naming the subject refuses the whole erasure (target: erase the unheld remainder and keep the held part restricted behind audited access, erased later only on a fresh preview and reconfirmation). A protected organization reset, which removes the organization's issuer and its last owner and sessions, leaves only anonymous receipts and outcomes (no name, email, profile, directory, destination identifier, signing key or credential) and sealed remote work pending at reset, sealed to the installation and never shown or exported in clear; both pass to installation owner custody alone. ## Honest boundaries and ownership

Auth owns the complete workforce identity job, including the private connectors, native software, provider app registrations and security engineering needed for it. Required provider permissions, licensed editions, certificate rotation, privacy agreements and operating work are connector deliverables, never reasons to omit a category requirement. Every integration names direction, supported operations, required permission and the point at which access is actually verified.

- **HR and payroll:** [Willow](https://github.com/just-every/run-willow) owns employee records, onboarding HR work, time off and HR documents. Auth consumes approved changes and returns access outcomes. Rippling, Gusto, BambooHR, Workday, ADP Workforce Now, UKG Pro, Paylocity, Namely and SAP SuccessFactors remain supported sources, and any other HR system feeds Auth by scheduled file (AUTH-HRIS-002); Auth does not run payroll, file W-2/1099 or taxes, administer benefits or absorb that unowned category.
- **Endpoint and network operations:** Auth owns directory/device identity, sign-in to the computer itself (AUTH-DESKTOP-001), posture-based access, LDAP/RADIUS, gateway access to on-premises web apps (AUTH-GATEWAY-001), server and SSH access (AUTH-PRIVILEGED-001) and the connector/native software needed to deliver them. Intune, Jamf Pro, JumpCloud, AD and VPN/Wi-Fi vendors supply broad endpoint management, EDR, patch deployment and network transport; Auth integrates and reports their trust evidence and access limits.
- **Secrets and application ownership:** Connector/signing credentials are protected installation secrets. Auth stores and fills app sign-in credentials for password-based apps (AUTH-SWA-001); it is not a personal password manager or a general secrets-management product for infrastructure secrets and API keys, which stay with 1Password, AWS Secrets Manager or HashiCorp Vault. It supplies identity, app-role assignments, scoped API credentials and permission decisions; app owners enforce business-resource rules and retain ownership of app data. No group/claim implies authority until its target enforces it.
- **Compliance and operations:** [Evidence](https://github.com/just-every/run-evidence) owns audit readiness and auditor workspaces. Auth supplies exportable control evidence and is the identity-provider connection of Vanta, Drata, Secureframe and Thoropass (AUTH-COMPLIANCE-001), without claiming SOC 2 certification; those platforms and Evidence run the control tests. [Uptime](https://github.com/just-every/run-uptime) owns external monitoring and [Oncall](https://github.com/just-every/run-oncall) paging; Datadog/Splunk/Sentinel own log analytics. Auth still owns visible durable outcomes and operation of its identity service.
- **Delivery and external custody:** Amazon SES/Base mail and Twilio deliver messages; licensed SaaS providers enforce remote account/session controls. Provider acceptance is not human receipt, completed access or verified revocation. Where a vendor cannot revoke a session, the employer sees the residual risk, expiry and manual/partner step. No universal compatibility, uptime promise, region guarantee, identity-document verification or telecom network is claimed without the declared integration and measurement.
- **Portability and issuer limits:** Base command and transport machinery is retained, while Auth replaces Base operator privilege with its own proof, private authorization, identity archive and grant-map policy. AUTH-TRANSFER-001 records the current narrower replacement; AUTH-TRANSFER-002, AUTH-ERASURE-001, AUTH-RETENTION-001 and AUTH-AUDIT-001 define the complete target. Copied Base erasure/portability source alone is not product acceptance. Host moves require reenrollment/re-linking; an archive cannot reproduce hardware private keys or reveal a later erasure journal it was never given.
