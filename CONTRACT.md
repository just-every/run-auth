# Auth target product contract

Target scope approved 16 October 2026. Auth is a full workforce identity and single sign-on provider for a US employer replacing **Okta Workforce Identity, Microsoft Entra ID or JumpCloud**: one company directory, passkey-first MFA, SAML/OIDC into the company’s SaaS and legacy apps, HR-driven provisioning/deprovisioning and governed access. It remains the one sign-in for the owner’s RER apps, an owner-run MIT product declaring `oidc-provider@1` without depending on that interface itself. USD, US employers and US employment/timezone norms are the first commercial context.

The buyer is the US IT/security owner who must get every employee and contractor into the right apps on day one, change access as their job changes, and prove it ended when they leave. Rippling IT (identity), authentik and Zitadel are the comparison set for modern adoption; the push-further clauses add bounded agents that finish and verify that work. This target is the product being built toward, not a description of today’s release. It changes no approved homepage, interface or runtime behavior.

The owner-approved workforce scope supersedes the earlier CIAM positioning in category research. Its useful table-stakes—SDKs, hosted/embedded flows, organizations, profile APIs, enterprise connections, token lifecycle and migration—are retained here as workforce/app interoperability, not used to turn Auth into a consumer application platform. No neighboring unowned category is absorbed.

## How to read this contract

Each named clause is a buyer promise with a public-door acceptance journey, repeated with status in ACCEPTANCE.md. The final status table is grounded in code-audit commit `cdd103e1ceeeedbacf52badf7a51bfc6b18296f6`, also the main revision read for this edition; no implementation changed since that audit. The input audit covers 64 inventory rows (10 done, 23 partial, 31 missing); clause counts below differ because clauses organize the whole workforce job. `done` means the bounded clause has implementation and cited automated/journey evidence, not hosted acceptance or certification. `partial` names delivered parts and absent target behavior. `missing` has no implementing product path. Target journeys for unfinished behavior are requirements, not reports of a test run.

Interactive administration by the administrator roles AUTH-DECISIONS-001 lists (owner, identity administrator, security/policy administrator, app administrator, help desk, auditor with export rights and every custom administrator role) retains the existing hardware passkey and fresh local email requirements. Everyday business decisions—a manager, app owner or group owner approving or denying access, a reviewer certifying access, an employee requesting it—are not administration: they take an ordinary employee passkey sign-in plus the step-up policy requires (AUTH-DECISIONS-001), and may be made from Slack or Microsoft Teams (AUTH-CHAT-001) except grants of privileged roles. Machine administration, such as Terraform or a nightly cleanup script, uses scoped admin API credentials published by a human under AUTH-ADMINAPI-001 and AUTH-TERRAFORM-001; it never impersonates interactive proof, and preparer keys (AUTH-ADMIN-001) remain propose-only. Existing RER client registrations retain AUTH-PROTOCOL-001 unchanged. Broader interoperability is a separate explicit profile under AUTH-OIDC-001 and AUTH-TOKENS-001.

## Retained identity and RER guarantees

- **AUTH-HOST-001 — Issuer host.** The host owns the issuer and passkeys. Choose it before linking apps. A host change is a provider move requiring the apps' owners to approve re-linking; it does not silently preserve an issuer or passkey origin. Fresh local setup selects localhost; explicit and saved hosts are preserved.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Choose localhost on fresh setup; repeat with an explicit/saved issuer; enroll a passkey and link an app. Attempt a host move and require a new app-owner approval rather than preserving issuer authority.

- **AUTH-PRIVACY-001 — Shared claims.** Auth shares each person's email and name with the apps they use. Under RER Managed, RER can sign link approvals for the account's Auth install; it receives people's data only as an ordinary linked app, for people who sign in to RER through it.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Sign in to two linked apps with different granted identity scopes; inspect UserInfo and tokens for only the permitted claims. Add a RER approval key and confirm it grants no user-data access without an ordinary app sign-in.

- **AUTH-RER-TRUST-001 — RER trust.** Auth accepts a RER link approval only when an active trusted key signed it (`typ: rer-link-approval+jwt`, `aud` this issuer, lifetime at most five minutes, unique `jti` consumed once), its origin and link key match the app document Auth pulls itself, and its `claim`, if any, names the Auth owner. Keys come from the `RER_LINK_APPROVAL_KEYS` setting of a RER-provisioned install, or from an owner or administrator on Sign-in methods with passkey and fresh local email proof. Withdrawal, by an administrator there or by RER with an assertion signed by that key when the account leaves Managed, ends the key for good. RER-made links are flagged and notified like token-made links. An install with no trusted key makes no request to RER.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Link using a correctly signed RER approval, then refuse wrong issuer/origin/key/claim, stale assertion and jti replay. Withdraw trust through both supported doors and prove the key cannot be restored or used again.

- **AUTH-IDENTITY-001 — Owner and sign-in proof.** Base bootstrap claims the owner but conveys no Auth privilege. Email-link proof completes once, only in the requesting browser. First passkey enrollment requires Auth email proof; administration and additional passkey enrollment require hardware passkey proof. Every enrollment, recovery approval or completion and administration write also requires this browser's Auth email confirmation within ten minutes, rechecked with the session's authority in the committing write; an enrollment challenge is valid only in the session that requested it. Recovery of anyone holding an administrator or other privileged role (AUTH-DECISIONS-001) requires another administrator's approval, a cancellable notified 72-hour delay, or the local emergency recovery token, and this rule is never relaxed for them. A non-privileged employee may also take the same-business-day help-desk path of AUTH-RECOVERY-003; today every person follows the administrator rule, which is stricter than the target and satisfies it.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Through the real email, WebAuthn and administration routes, prove same-browser one-use confirmation, first/additional enrollment, fresh local email step-up rechecked at commit, wrong-session and revoked-session refusal, and approved/delayed/cancelled/emergency recovery.

- **AUTH-PROTOCOL-001 — OpenID Connect profile.** Auth implements code/PKCE with state, nonce, RFC 9207 issuer, private_key_jwt clients, pairwise subjects and ID tokens accompanied by signed bearer access tokens. Access tokens are restricted to Auth UserInfo, bound to client, subject, session and granted scopes, and valid for at most five minutes or the remaining session lifetime. UserInfo supports GET and POST, returns only scoped identity claims and rechecks active session, account, client, grant, email proof and domain policy. ID tokens and other bearer credentials cannot authorize UserInfo. No refresh tokens or offline access are offered for the static code-flow profiles. Existing clients retain ES256; clients may select RS256. Both algorithms’ keys are encrypted under AUTH_ENCRYPTION_KEY, published before use and retained through outstanding token expiry. These guarantees are exercised by the edition tests; they are not a certification statement.

  **Acceptance evidence:** A registered RER client completes code/PKCE/private_key_jwt, validates nonce/issuer/audience/pairwise subject, and calls scoped GET/POST UserInfo. Refuse code/assertion replay and inactive client/person/session/grant/policy; test encrypted ES256/RS256 publish-before-use and retirement.

- **AUTH-BOUNDARY-001 — Private authorization.** Every private document and API enforces its own session and administrative policy. The seed denies inherited Base operator, invitation, provider and domain management paths. Only bootstrap, Base session inspection and local Base sign-out remain exposed; gated dev entry exists exclusively in the development bundle. Removing the gate removes dev-session authority; production refuses dev proof.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Request each private page/API signed out, as an email-only member, as a passkey administrator and with revoked authority; deny inherited Base management and production dev tokens. Remove the local dev gate and confirm authority ends.

- **AUTH-MOVEIN-001 — Staged move-in.** Directory exports are staged apart from people: a staged row cannot sign in, receives no mail and appears only on the move-in review. Admission creates the same invited member as a People invitation, with no email proof, administrator right, password, factor, app role, group permission or session, and sends mail only when the owner chooses to send invitations. A matching email never rebinds an existing person, passkey or upstream link. Undo removes only people the batch admitted who have never signed in and hold no passkey, app grant or later activity, and records who it removed and kept. An Auth0 former subject is data for app owners: no protocol endpoint accepts it and it never replaces the pairwise subject. Staging, admission, invitation and undo are administration writes under AUTH-IDENTITY-001.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Stage Google, Entra, Auth0, Okta and JumpCloud exports through Move-in, inspect collision/disabled rows, admit selected members without proof/roles/mail, explicitly invite, and undo only untouched admissions. Refuse former Auth0 subjects as credentials and concurrent/stale administrative authority.

- **AUTH-ADMIN-001 — Agent preparation.** An agent prepares administration and a person decides. A preparer key, minted, renewed and revoked by an administrator under AUTH-IDENTITY-001, is shown once, stored as a digest, expires 30 days after minting or renewal, and is accepted only by the preparer API while unrevoked and while its accountable administrator remains the administrator who minted it: it ends for good when that person is demoted, suspended or removed, and promoting or restoring them again does not bring it back (a new key is minted). It reads an allow-listed inventory (no session, passkey, key or token material) and files proposals to suspend or restore a person, invite a person, remove an invitation, or add or remove a domain's required provider; filing stores the affected people and apps and a digest of the target's state, and changes nothing else. A proposal runs only on one exact approval: an administrator's passkey signature over a challenge naming it, with that session's fresh local email proof, rechecked with the approver's authority, the unchanged target state and an unexpired, undecided proposal in the committing write. It runs once; it never suspends the owner or its own approver; its outcome is recorded in Activity with the key and the approver. A preparer key confers no session, page, approval, key management or protocol access and is not an access token.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Mint/renew/revoke a preparer key, inspect its allow-listed inventory, file each supported proposal and approve its exact state with a person’s passkey and fresh local email. Prove expiry, stale-state refusal, one winner under concurrent approvals and permanent loss of key authority after administrator removal.

- **CMD-001 — Commands.** Auth retains the shared RER command dispatcher and verbs. Its edition-owned extensions are documented in AUTH-COMMANDS.md. Setup applies real D1 migrations and claims the first owner. Sign-in begins in the browser, never by presenting an unusable CLI-bound proof. Local recovery writes only a token digest to D1 and stores the raw token in a mode-0600 private file.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Run real install/setup/dev, start browser sign-in, repeat setup without replacing owner/settings/data, stop the managed process, and generate/redeem local recovery with only a digest in D1 and a mode-0600 raw token file.

- **HTTP-001 — HTTP surfaces.** Hono serves health/API dispatch and Start serves remaining documents; Auth intercepts identity, protocol and private Auth pages before dispatch. Unknown routes return 404.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Call health, Auth APIs and public/private documents through the emitted Worker; confirm identity interception, per-request authorization and an ordinary 404 for unknown routes.

- **AUTH-LINK-001 — App links.** Auth links apps through Base's pull protocol (LINKING.md). It reads only the app's own documents at its exact origin and binds the exact callback set. Codes are one-use, last 60 seconds and are redeemed atomically. It verifies the redeem assertion (audience, at most 60 seconds, unique `jti`). A client keeps its id and sector. A new key needs an administrator who saw the existing link, and a link replaces another app (its sector at another origin, or another sector at its origin) only on an approval that named it. Link tokens cover one origin and callback set, are single use and last at most an hour. Production installs refuse loopback and plain-http origins; dev installs serve only loopback apps. Token- and RER-made links are flagged and announced. Reported interface versions are kept, and a lower one, reported or re-linked, waits for the owner. Removing an app signs out everyone it signed in.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Use the pull/link-token flow to link a real Base app Worker, sign in, re-link with continuity, and remove it. Refuse mismatched callbacks/origins, replay/concurrent redemption, unauthorized key swap and unapproved downgrade; observe linked-app sessions end on removal.

- **AUTH-LOGOUT-001 — Sign-out reach.** Sign out everywhere, suspension (identity fence) and RP-initiated logout send signed `logout+jwt` tokens with a unique `jti` and no `nonce` to each linked app's registered logout path. Each is retried for up to 24 hours. Discovery advertises `end_session_endpoint` and back-channel logout with session support.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Sign out everywhere, suspend a person and request RP logout through real Auth/app endpoints; the app rejects the old session and replayed logout tokens. Exercise bounded retries and inspect the persisted delivery outcome; discovery advertises the supported endpoints.

- **AUTH-TRANSFER-001 — Export and import.** Export carries people, emails, passkey public keys, clients, grants, subjects, history and a signed grant map. It omits sessions, private keys, the pairwise key and the RER keys an owner trusted; withdrawn RER keys stay withdrawn. Import refuses app origins the destination cannot serve and active dev personas. A restore at the same host keeps every client, sector and pairwise subject.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Export a populated installation, import into an empty installation at the same host, and re-link the app with unchanged client/sector/pairwise subject. Inspect retained records and absent authority; refuse incompatible origins and active dev personas.

## The workforce directory and employee experience

- **AUTH-DIRECTORY-001 — One workforce directory.** Employers manage employees, contractors, service identities, managers, departments, locations, employment dates, immutable person/source identifiers, custom attributes and active/invited/suspended/terminated states. Search, filter, bulk preview and admin API edits (made through AUTH-ADMINAPI-001 credentials or an administrator's session) use the same validation and audit. Static and attribute-driven groups have explainable membership, group owners and mapped app roles; source precedence and manual overrides are visible. Rehires and email/name changes preserve reviewed identity continuity rather than merging by email.

  **Acceptance evidence:** In People/API, onboard a W-2 employee and a contractor, rename and transfer one, preview a bulk edit, evaluate dynamic groups, suspend and rehire; verify stable identifiers, explicit source conflicts and resulting assignments without duplicate accounts.

- **AUTH-TENANCY-001 — Organizations and delegated administration.** An installation can serve multiple isolated employer organizations, subsidiaries and partner workspaces. People may hold distinct memberships and roles in more than one, with an explicit active organization. Owner, identity administrator, app administrator, group owner, manager, help desk, auditor and employee permissions are scoped and server-enforced, including exports and API credentials; each built-in role's permissions are listed, and employers compose further roles and scopes under AUTH-ROLES-001 (custom roles, resource sets of groups/apps/people, separate view and edit scope, no action on a holder of a higher role). Invitations expire, can be cancelled, and never grant more than the inviter can delegate. Employers can delegate setup without exposing another employer’s directory or issuer secrets.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Invite a contractor into two employers, switch organization, delegate one app to an app administrator and one recovery case to help desk; deny cross-organization reads/writes/exports and unauthorized role escalation, including direct API requests.

- **AUTH-ROLES-001 — Custom administrator roles and resource sets.** Employers build custom administrator roles from a published permission list (for example: view people, edit profile fields, suspend/restore, reset factors, approve recovery, manage group membership, manage group rules, configure app, assign app, manage policy, manage identity providers, view audit, export audit, manage admin API credentials). A role is granted over a resource set: named groups, attribute-defined groups (such as "non-privileged users in the US group"), apps and individual people, with view scope and edit scope set separately. The built-in AUTH-TENANCY-001 roles are expressible in the same model. Server enforcement applies to UI, admin API and exports alike: no scoped role can act on a person who holds an administrator role equal to or higher than its own, nor on any member of a group outside its edit scope, and a role holder can never grant a role or resource set wider than their own. Role and resource-set changes are administrator writes under AUTH-IDENTITY-001, previewed with who gains and loses which actions, and audited.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Create a help-desk role with "reset factors" over group A and view-only over group B. As its holder, reset a factor for a person in A; be refused editing in B (view succeeds), refused on an administrator in A, and refused via direct API and export. Attempt to grant the role over a wider set and be refused; change group A's membership and observe the next decision follow it.

- **AUTH-DOMAINS-001 — Verified employer domains.** Administrators prove control of a corporate domain through expiring DNS or equivalent ownership challenges before domain-based enrollment, discovery or federation routing is activated. A domain cannot be silently claimed by another employer. Preview shows who a rule affects, aliases are explicit, and removal/reverification cannot strand emergency owners. Verified domain control never by itself proves an individual’s identity or licenses account linking.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Claim a domain through the admin setup door, fail an incorrect/expired challenge and competing claim, then enroll and route an eligible worker. Revoke domain ownership and confirm policy reevaluation and audited recovery access.

- **AUTH-PROFILE-001 — Profiles and safe identity linking.** Employees can read and edit permitted profile fields through account UI/API; administrators manage typed custom metadata with field-level visibility and source ownership. Linking or unlinking Google, Microsoft, Okta, GitHub, Apple or generic upstream identities requires proof of the existing account and the new identity, explicit confirmation and a surviving sign-in/recovery path. An issuer/subject pair has one binding; matching email, imported metadata or an agent suggestion alone cannot take over an account.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Edit an employee profile and protected HR field, connect a second proven upstream and then unlink it; refuse an email collision, foreign subject, concurrent binding and last usable sign-in removal. Inspect app claims for only consented fields.

- **AUTH-ENROLLMENT-001 — Managed enrollment and password compatibility.** Invitation, approved-domain self-registration and HR-driven prehire flows share verification, duplicate resolution, terms/consent and recovery controls. Passkeys are the default. Employers migrating password-dependent workforces can explicitly enable hashed, breached-password-screened password sign-in/reset and email or SMS one-time verification; these methods cannot confer hardware administration proof or bypass conditional access. Public registration stays closed unless employer policy opens it, and a pending invitation or import is never authenticated authority.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Enroll an invited employee and an approved-domain self-registrant, reject an outside address, complete password reset and a one-use SMS/email challenge, and prove weaker methods cannot administer or bypass required MFA. Cancel enrollment and verify its outstanding links stop.

- **AUTH-EXPERIENCE-001 — Hosted and embedded identity journeys.** Employers configure an accessible branded sign-in, enrollment, account and recovery experience on their chosen verified host. React/Next.js components embed the same server-enforced user, membership and organization controls; a versioned journey editor composes approved verification and step-up steps without arbitrary client-side authorization. Preview, test and rollback preserve anti-enumeration, trusted redirects, emergency access and the strict RER profile.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Configure branding and an employee journey, use both hosted and embedded account/membership screens, preview a changed MFA step and roll it back. At 1440 and 390, in light/dark with keyboard and assistive technology, complete sign-in/recovery and expose durable errors without leaking hidden fields.

- **AUTH-LAUNCHER-001 — Employee app launcher.** Every employee gets a searchable dashboard of assigned SaaS, legacy and RER apps—SAML, OIDC, and password-based apps signed in through the Auth browser extension (AUTH-SWA-001)—with favorites, recently used apps and clear access-request actions. Each tile opens the correct employer/role and explains an unprovisioned, denied, expired or connector-blocked assignment. Unassigned apps do not yield working launch assertions or filled credentials; app owners can publish instructions for apps requiring a separate client or VPN.

  **Acceptance evidence:** As an employee with Google Workspace, Slack, AWS, a password-only app and a RER app assigned, launch each into its proper account. Request an unassigned app, remove an assignment and expire a temporary grant; the dashboard, the extension and direct launch all enforce the changed access.

- **AUTH-SWA-001 — Password-based app sign-in.** Apps whose plan has no SAML or OIDC are first-class launcher apps. An Auth browser extension for Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari (macOS and iOS) recognizes the app's sign-in page from its recipe, fills and submits the credential only on the recipe's exact origins, and offers the same on direct visits. App administrators choose per app between admin-set shared credentials (employees never see the password) and user-set individual credentials, and may let Auth generate strong passwords to a recipe's length/character rules and rotate them. Credentials are encrypted per person under keys Auth never sends to model context, logs or exports; shared credentials are released only to currently assigned people, and reveal, copy and change are policy-controlled and audited. Unassigning or offboarding a person removes their access in the extension immediately, and the leaver plan (AUTH-LIFECYCLE-001) rotates every shared credential they could use and records each password-only app as an access obligation until rotated or confirmed removed. AUTH-MIGRATION-001 imports Okta SWA app definitions (URL, field selectors, credential scheme) and leaves credentials to re-entry or a guided admin import. This is app sign-in, not a general personal password manager.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Install the extension in each of the four browsers, assign a shared-credential and an individual-credential app, sign in from the launcher and by direct visit, generate a password and refuse fill on a lookalike origin. Unassign one employee and offboard another; their next fill is refused within 60 seconds, the shared password is rotated, and the audit shows every reveal, fill and rotation without the secret. Import an Okta SWA app definition and launch it.

## Authentication, SSO and policy

- **AUTH-MFA-001 — Passkey-first multifactor policy.** Employers require phishing-resistant platform/synced passkeys or hardware security keys, offer TOTP and single-use recovery codes under explicit assurance rules, and control enrollment, replacement and factor loss. SMS/email are policy-limited bootstrap/recovery alternatives with lower assurance, never equivalent to a security key. The Auth mobile authenticator (AUTH-AUTHENTICATOR-001) adds push with number matching and its own TOTP; each factor's assurance (phishing-resistant, push, TOTP, SMS/email) is published and policy chooses which satisfy which app, RADIUS flow (AUTH-LEGACY-001) and OS login (AUTH-DESKTOP-001). Factor inventory, lost-factor revocation, step-up and employee notifications cover web, desktop and native mobile; a lost factor without a recovery code is restored the same business day under AUTH-RECOVERY-003. Existing hardware-passkey and fresh local email gates for interactive Auth administration by the roles AUTH-DECISIONS-001 lists remain mandatory.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Enroll a platform passkey, hardware key, Auth push and TOTP, require step-up for a sensitive app, revoke a lost factor and consume a recovery code once. Deny downgraded admin access and prove factor removal/rotation reaches current sessions according to policy.

- **AUTH-AUTHENTICATOR-001 — Push authenticator in the Auth mobile app.** The iOS and Android Auth app (AUTH-MOBILE-001) enrolls as an authenticator bound to a device key held in the Secure Enclave/StrongBox and protected by biometrics or device PIN. It delivers push approvals showing the requesting app, employee, approximate location, IP, device and time, with number matching (the employee types the number shown on the sign-in screen) wherever the requesting flow can display one; it also generates TOTP offline. Push carries no approval in the notification payload itself: approval is a signed response from the enrolled device key, single use and bound to the transaction, expiring after 120 seconds. Denying a push refuses the sign-in and optionally reports it as suspicious, which notifies the security owner and can suspend further pushes to that person (push-fatigue control: rate limit and automatic number matching after repeated denials). Phishing-resistant passkeys remain the default for browser sign-in; push and TOTP are the stated factors for RADIUS/VPN, Wi-Fi and other flows where a passkey cannot cross the protocol, and policy can require a passkey instead wherever one can. Lost-device removal invalidates the device key at once.

  **Acceptance evidence:** On real iOS and Android builds, enroll the authenticator, approve a web sign-in with number matching, deny another and see it refused and reported, and complete a sign-in with the app's offline TOTP. Replay an approval, answer after expiry and answer from a second unenrolled device; each is refused. Send repeated pushes and observe the fatigue control; remove the device and prove its next approval is refused.

- **AUTH-POLICY-001 — Conditional access and session policy.** Versioned policies combine employee/group/app/role, authentication assurance, managed-device posture, IP/network/location, sign-in risk and session age. Administrators simulate impact before publishing; an explainable decision records matched rule and evidence freshness. Deny or require step-up when posture is missing/stale, enforce idle/absolute session lifetime and fresh authentication on critical actions, and reevaluate when membership, location, device or policy changes. Emergency accounts are explicitly bounded, monitored and drilled rather than a blanket bypass.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Preview and publish a payroll-app rule requiring a managed US device and fresh passkey; attempt allowed, foreign-network, stale-posture and expired-session launches through app and direct protocol routes. Change group/policy during sign-in and verify the current decision at token issuance.

- **AUTH-ABUSE-001 — Automated attack protection.** Configurable bot challenges, suspicious-IP/device throttling, credential-stuffing and brute-force detection, breached-password screening, lockout and user/security-team notification protect every enrollment, sign-in, reset and token endpoint. Decisions are tenant-scoped and observable, avoid account enumeration and do not let a remote attacker permanently lock out emergency recovery. Risk-driven step-up and unlock retain the same account proof and administrator controls.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Attack real authentication/reset/token HTTP doors with bot traffic, password spraying, a breached password and distributed source attempts; observe rate limits/lockouts, protected notifications and legitimate recovery. Simulate a risk change and verify step-up and an explainable log.

- **AUTH-SAML-001 — SAML 2.0 workforce identity provider.** Auth supplies SP-initiated and explicitly permitted IdP-initiated SAML SSO for company apps, with per-app metadata import/export, entity IDs, exact ACS allow-lists, NameID/attribute/group/role mappings (AUTH-MAPPING-001) and certificate rollover with overlapping publication. Every release supports at minimum: HTTP-POST and HTTP-Redirect bindings for AuthnRequest and Response; RSA-SHA256 and RSA-SHA512 signatures, chosen per app, on the response, the assertion or both; optional assertion encryption with AES-256-GCM or AES-128-CBC content encryption and RSA-OAEP key transport; SP-initiated and IdP-initiated Single Logout over HTTP-Redirect and HTTP-POST; and the `persistent`, `emailAddress`, `unspecified` and `transient` NameID formats. RSA-SHA1 is accepted only as a named per-app compatibility exception. Auth validates request signatures where configured, destination, audience, request correlation, time windows (at most 5 minutes' clock skew) and replay; setup exposes incompatible profiles rather than claiming universal SAML compatibility.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Configure a generic SAML SP through the admin door, launch as an assigned employee over each binding, validate mapped role/NameID in each of the four formats, sign the response, the assertion and both with SHA-256 and SHA-512, decrypt an encrypted assertion with each named algorithm at the SP, and roll certificates with overlap. Complete SP- and IdP-initiated logout over both bindings. Refuse a forged request, altered ACS, wrong audience, SHA-1 without an exception, stale/replayed assertion and unassigned user.

- **AUTH-OIDC-001 — General SaaS OIDC and OAuth profiles.** Alongside the unchanged AUTH-PROTOCOL-001 RER profile, app administrators register ordinary SaaS/web/native OIDC clients with discovery, exact redirects, the authorization code flow, scopes, claim mappings (AUTH-MAPPING-001) and per-app public or pairwise subjects. Supported client authentication methods are listed in discovery and chosen per client: `client_secret_basic`, `client_secret_post`, `private_key_jwt`, and `none` for public clients, which must use PKCE (S256); confidential clients may also be required to use PKCE. Client secrets are generated by Auth with at least 256 bits, shown once, stored hashed, and rotated with an overlap window. The implicit and hybrid flows are available only as a named per-app opt-in for legacy SaaS that requires them. None of this loosens existing RER registrations, which keep `private_key_jwt` and mandatory PKCE. Generic clients receive documented consent, session, UserInfo, logout (RP-initiated, front-channel and back-channel) and key-rotation semantics; denied grants cannot mint tokens.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Register confidential SaaS clients with each of `client_secret_basic`, `client_secret_post` and `private_key_jwt`, and a public native client with `none` plus PKCE, through the catalog/API; complete code flows and logout for each and rotate a secret with overlap. Refuse a wrong or rotated-out secret, a public client without PKCE, redirect substitution, token substitution, code replay and unsupported grants. Re-run the strict RER journey unchanged and inspect each connection’s advertised profile.

- **AUTH-MAPPING-001 — Attribute and NameID transform language.** Per-app SAML attributes, NameID and OIDC claims, SCIM/API attribute pushes and generated usernames are computed by a documented, versioned expression language over the person's directory, source and group data. It offers string functions (concatenate, lower/upper case, substring, replace, regular-expression replace, trim, pad), conditionals and null handling, date formatting, group-membership tests and list filters, and lookups of the person's manager and source fields—enough to express, for example, a `first.last` username or an app role chosen from group membership. Expressions are typed, side-effect free, cannot read secrets or make network calls, and are bounded in run time. The editor previews each expression's output for chosen people before saving, flags people for whom it yields empty or duplicate values, and every change is versioned and audited.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Through the app setup door, map NameID to a computed `first.last` value with a collision suffix, map a role from group membership and push a formatted date over SCIM; preview against three people including one with a missing field, then launch and provision and read the values back at the SP and SCIM service. Refuse an expression that reads a secret, calls out or exceeds the time bound; roll back to the previous version.

## SaaS, directories and workforce lifecycle

- **AUTH-CATALOG-001 — Production SaaS application catalog.** A maintained catalog configures Google Workspace, Microsoft 365, Slack, GitHub Enterprise, AWS IAM Identity Center, Salesforce and Zoom for SAML/OIDC SSO and supported lifecycle APIs/SCIM. Each recipe states required vendor edition/permissions, groups/roles, attribute ownership, test-user procedure, secret/certificate rotation and deactivation/session limits. Administrators can clone a recipe into a generic connector, securely enter provider credentials, test, preview changes, approve and roll back. A catalog label alone is not a working integration. These seven are the deep-journey anchors; catalog breadth is AUTH-CATALOG-002 and Microsoft 365 domain federation is AUTH-M365-001.

  **Acceptance evidence:** For each of the seven named services, an authorized US test tenant connects from catalog, assigns a test group/role, creates or binds the correct account, launches SSO, changes role and offboards it; read back remote state and document any external-session limit. Inject one provider denial and prove it remains unresolved.

- **AUTH-EVENTS-001 — Reliable lifecycle events and webhooks.** Authorized subscribers receive versioned signed events for people, memberships, assignments, policy, recovery and provisioning outcomes. Delivery uses stable IDs, bounded retries/backoff, secret rotation, replay protection and per-subscriber outcomes; consumers can resume from a cursor and reconcile missed events through the API. Destinations are validated against SSRF/private-network abuse. A delivery acknowledgement proves receipt only, and uncertain writes/events do not falsely close downstream access obligations.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Subscribe a real app endpoint through admin/API, perform a joiner/mover/leaver, rotate signing material and recover after 429, timeout and outage. Replay an event and request a historical cursor; verify ordered/versioned reconstruction without duplicate grants or missing unresolved work.

- **AUTH-FOLLOWUP-001 — Follow-through until access is resolved.** Enrollment, recovery, app setup, provisioning, access requests, reviews and revocation each retain an owner, next due action, employer timezone/business calendar, deadline and stop/escalation rule. Email, Slack and Microsoft Teams reminders go to approved recipients, who can decide from Slack and Teams under AUTH-CHAT-001; Oncall can receive security escalation. Connector denial, stale source, missing approval and uncertain remote effect stay visible across restart, with actionable redacted errors. Satisfied/cancelled/expired authority stops reminders; a sent alert or successful tool call alone never means the employee can sign in or has lost access.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Leave an invitation, access approval and failed deprovision unresolved across a weekend/DST change and process restart; observe due actions/reminders/escalation, complete or cancel each through its normal door, and verify reminders cease while the complete history remains.

- **AUTH-DELIVERY-001 — Email and SMS delivery operations.** Employers use configured Base mail adapters or Amazon SES with verified sending identities for invitations, verification, recovery and security notices; Twilio supplies optional SMS verification. Private credentials, scoped templates, bounce/suppression handling, rate limits and durable delivery/uncertainty outcomes protect these messages. Admin diagnostics distinguish queued, provider-accepted and delivered/bounced evidence without exposing a usable link/token; local outbox remains private and sends no external mail. Provider failure does not grant identity proof.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Configure SES and Twilio in authorized test accounts, enroll/recover a worker and observe accepted, bounced, throttled and unavailable outcomes. Prove tokens/credentials absent from logs/exports and a notification failure leaves the original access obligation unresolved.

- **AUTH-OPS-001 — Operate a company identity service.** Owners can deploy, upgrade, monitor, back up and recover a US-first identity installation with explicit issuer/key custody, US data-region choices, maintenance/incident runbooks, capacity and recoverable connector state. The published objectives for the supported production deployment (Cloudflare Workers with D1, as `deploy.json` defines it) are: 99.99% monthly availability of sign-in, token, SAML, WS-Federation, RADIUS and launcher endpoints and 99.9% of the admin console; recovery time objective 1 hour and recovery point objective 5 minutes for a regional failure or a restore from backup; p95 server-side latency at a US edge of at most 300 ms for an authorization or SAML response and at most 200 ms for a token or UserInfo response, excluding the person's own interaction and upstream/push wait; and RADIUS responses within 2 seconds plus the person's push response. Measurements over each month are published on the operations page with the method used. Uptime monitors sign-in/provisioning health and Oncall receives actionable incidents. Administrators see active-worker, app, SMS/email and connector usage with USD price/cost rules and exportable forecasts before enabling billable providers; estimates distinguish measured usage and provider charges. No certification, service level or hosted capacity is claimed without measurement.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Deploy to the supported production host, perform a rolling/key-certificate upgrade, fail a dependency, restore a backup and measure against each numeric objective: availability from synthetic sign-ins at one-minute intervals across a month, the restore's elapsed time against the 1-hour RTO and its newest recovered write against the 5-minute RPO, p95 authorization/SAML and token/UserInfo latency under a load of 50 sign-ins per second, and leaver processing against AUTH-TIMING-001. Trigger Uptime/Oncall escalation and reconcile the USD usage forecast to actual provider usage without exposing secrets.

- **AUTH-RECOVERY-002 — Emergency continuity and recovery drills.** Employers maintain separately protected emergency administrators, redundant factors/keys and documented issuer, upstream-outage, lost-factor and connector recovery paths. Scheduled drills prove those paths with scoped test identities, without bypassing the existing local email/hardware gates or turning an offline archive into active authority. High-risk recovery changes require independent approval, notify the affected employee/security owner and are cancellable where a delay applies; rotation after use is explicit. A drill reports failed and unmeasured steps.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Drill lost phone/key, unavailable upstream, expired signing certificate and restoration of an owner installation through the public account/CLI doors. Confirm ordinary workers cannot invoke emergency authority, cancellation wins against completion where applicable, all notices persist and used credentials cannot be replayed.

- **AUTH-RECOVERY-003 — Same-day help-desk recovery for employees.** A non-privileged employee who has lost every factor and holds no recovery code is restored the same business day through help desk. A help-desk holder whose AUTH-ROLES-001 scope covers the employee opens a recovery case and records a verification step the employer's policy accepts: attestation by the employee's manager from the manager's own signed-in session, a remaining enrolled factor (push or TOTP), or an ID-document and selfie check through Persona or CLEAR, whose result (not the document) is stored. On a passed check, help desk resets the employee's factors and issues a single-use enrollment link or desktop recovery PIN (AUTH-DESKTOP-001), valid for at most 1 hour and bound to the case; all the employee's sessions and devices are revoked, and the employee and their manager are notified on every enrolled channel and by email. There is no 72-hour wait for these employees. The path is refused for anyone holding an administrator or privileged role (AUTH-DECISIONS-001), who stay on AUTH-IDENTITY-001's approval, delay or emergency rules, and for anyone outside the help-desk holder's scope. Help desk's own action is under the administrator gate, and every case records who verified what, how and when.

  **Acceptance evidence:** As help desk scoped to the employee's group, restore a non-admin employee with no recovery code through manager attestation, and another through a Persona check in an authorized test account, each from case opening to a newly enrolled passkey in under 1 hour. Refuse the path for an administrator, for an employee outside scope, without a recorded verification, and on reuse or after an hour of the enrollment link; see the revoked sessions and every notice.

## Privacy, custody and Base replacements

- **AUTH-ERASURE-001 — Authorized erasure with retained accountability.** Owners erase a person, organization, app connection or installation under explicit retention/legal-hold policy, after preview and confirmation. Erasure removes profiles, factor keys, invitations, grants, staged/import copies, connector credentials and dependent personal data, revokes external authority, and reports unfinished remote removal separately. Durable audit/decision evidence uses anonymized references with a per-store completion receipt and portability fence; restore cannot resurrect erased records when given the later erasure journal. Already delivered messages and independently held provider data have named limits and partner deletion instructions. Suspension alone is not erasure; the last owner requires a protected reset/transfer path.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Erase a worker with multiple factors, imports, agent grants and SaaS access through the owner door; query/export all stores, inspect receipt and anonymized audit, and attempt restore of a pre-erasure archive with the later journal. Keep a blocked remote deletion visible, test legal hold and last-owner protection, then reset a local installation.

- **AUTH-RETENTION-001 — Explicit retention and secret custody.** The product publishes employer-controlled retention for profiles, source snapshots, authentication/decision history, provisioning obligations, audit, model traces and erasure receipts, including retention holds and US storage/processor choices. Out of the box, sign-in, administration and decision audit is kept for 13 months, configurable up to 7 years; the employer can never set it below 90 days, and no event is deleted while an open access review, access request, investigation or legal hold refers to its period. Before any scheduled deletion, the employer can export what will be deleted. Expiry of short-lived challenge/rate-limit/token state cannot delete the only evidence of an unfinished obligation or improve a completion report. Encryption, scoped access and rotation protect signing/connector secrets at rest and in transit; raw credentials and authentication links never enter model context, ordinary logs, public assets or portable archives.

  **Acceptance evidence:** On a default installation, confirm 13-month audit retention; try to set it to 30 days and be refused; open a review over a period and see its events kept past their expiry. Populate every retained class, age it across each configured expiry, restart and inspect the public history/export doors: transient secrets expire, retained history and unresolved obligations remain, held data is preserved and scheduled deletion produces accountable results. Rotate a connector/signing key and verify secrets absent from logs/assets/archive.

- **AUTH-TRANSFER-002 — Complete workforce portability.** Beyond AUTH-TRANSFER-001’s current identity archive, versioned export includes organizations, workforce attributes, groups, source/subject maps, app recipes/assignments, policies, access requests/reviews, lifecycle obligations/outcomes, delegated-grant definitions, audit and erasure journals. Import previews validation/conflicts and restores inactive into an empty authorized destination; no app send, provisioning write, trusted agent or session starts until the receiving owner reauthorizes. Installation-local signing/wrapping keys, connector credentials, refresh/bearer tokens and passkey private material never travel. Same-host public passkey/subject continuity and cross-host reenrollment/re-link limits are explicit.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Create a populated workforce installation, export/import/export through rer and compare every declared portable record. Refuse corrupt/foreign/unknown schema and nonempty destination; restore with later erasure journal, confirm all jobs/grants are inactive, then reauthorize one connector and verify exactly its approved work resumes.

## Push further: agents serving workforce identity

- **AUTH-AGENT-001 — Agent-led SaaS onboarding.** With an app owner’s scoped consent, an agent reads vendor metadata, current catalog recipes and employer group/role policy, prepares SAML/OIDC and SCIM settings, proposes mappings and configures approved changes through actual vendor APIs or guided setup. Credentials stay in connector custody, never model text. Domain trust, new scopes, role elevation, destructive changes and activation need exact human approval over the proposed diff; observed test-user sign-in, role change and revocation determine readiness. The agent chases missing decisions and preserves rollback plus failed/unverified steps.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Ask the agent to onboard Slack and a generic SAML/SCIM SaaS, approve a scoped configuration diff, and run a real assigned employee through sign-in/provision/change/revoke. Inject malicious vendor text, stale settings and withheld domain consent; prove no unauthorized change and a blocked readiness state with an actionable next step.

- **AUTH-AGENT-002 — Diagnose and repair broken sign-in.** An agent correlates approved redacted protocol traces, metadata/key/certificate state, policy decisions, mail outcomes and deployment/configuration changes to explain why a named worker cannot use an app. It proposes the smallest justified repair, tests it with a scoped test identity and verifies sign-in plus logout. Changes to issuer, redirects, scopes, certificates, factors or policy require the responsible owner’s exact approval; it cannot turn off security controls, infer proof from logs or read raw secrets. Uncertain diagnosis stays uncertain with a next diagnostic action.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Break a callback, expire a certificate and fail recovery mail on separate app journeys; ask the agent to diagnose with source citations, approve a repair and verify end-to-end behavior. Deny approval and inject untrusted trace instructions, confirming no configuration or authority changes.

- **AUTH-AGENT-003 — Find orphaned and excessive access.** An agent joins HR employment/source state, directory groups, app-side assignments, observed usage and review history to identify orphaned accounts, role drift, dormant privileges and risky recovery changes. Findings show provenance, freshness, exact people/apps affected and counterevidence; missing use data never proves no need. Policy owners approve least-privilege changes against a current-state diff; the agent verifies actual removal and that affected workers can still do their approved jobs, with an explicit rollback/exception route.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Feed real approved connector data containing a departed worker’s remote account, an excessive role and an unused but required emergency grant. Review the cited findings, approve only the first two, and verify downstream state plus the worker’s allowed tasks; stale data or changed employment invalidates the proposal.

- **AUTH-AGENT-004 — Person-bound delegated agent identities.** People can consent to an OAuth/MCP or external-API task identity bound to accountable person, employer, agent, purpose, target resource/API, exact scopes, duration and expiry. A visible consent screen explains the concrete effects, required independent approvals and revocation reach; tokens are short-lived, target-bound and separately attributable, never reusable human sessions or preparer keys. New scopes/purpose/targets and privilege escalation require new explicit consent; employer/person/agent suspension, task completion or expiry ends authority. Untrusted app/document text cannot enlarge it.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Authorize an agent to update one approved app assignment until a fixed deadline, invoke its target API/MCP tool and inspect attribution. Deny another person, purpose, resource, target, scope, expired grant and replay/substitution; show and revoke the active grant in My account and prove its next request is refused.

- **AUTH-AGENT-005 — Verify revocation everywhere.** On any human or agent grant ending, an agent inventories every issued derivative credential, session and provisioned role across the affected apps, sends only approved revocation operations and checks remote state or a safe denied-access probe. The UI distinguishes locally denied, provider-acknowledged, verified revoked, unsupported and unknown. Expiry/offboarding cannot be reported fully complete until every in-scope effect is verified or a named accountable owner accepts a documented exception under policy; unsupported vendor APIs produce an actionable manual/partner step, never fabricated success.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Revoke a delegated grant and offboard its accountable worker while one SaaS is offline and another offers no session-revocation API. Prove immediate Auth denial, enumerate all derivatives, resume verification after restart/provider recovery and retain the manual exception until a person resolves it; every final claim must match a remote read/probe.

## Honest boundaries and ownership

Auth owns the complete workforce identity job, including the private connectors, native software, provider app registrations and security engineering needed for it. Required provider permissions, licensed editions, certificate rotation, privacy agreements and operating work are connector deliverables, never reasons to omit a category requirement. Every integration names direction, supported operations, required permission and the point at which access is actually verified.

- **HR and payroll:** [Willow](https://github.com/just-every/run-willow) owns employee records, onboarding HR work, time off and HR documents. Auth consumes approved changes and returns access outcomes. Rippling, Gusto, BambooHR, Workday, ADP Workforce Now, UKG Pro, Paylocity, Namely and SAP SuccessFactors remain supported sources, and any other HR system feeds Auth by scheduled file (AUTH-HRIS-002); Auth does not run payroll, file W-2/1099 or taxes, administer benefits or absorb that unowned category.
- **Endpoint and network operations:** Auth owns directory/device identity, sign-in to the computer itself (AUTH-DESKTOP-001), posture-based access, LDAP/RADIUS and the connector/native software needed to deliver them. Intune, Jamf Pro, JumpCloud, AD and VPN/Wi-Fi vendors supply broad endpoint management, EDR, patch deployment and network transport; Auth integrates and reports their trust evidence and access limits.
- **Secrets and application ownership:** Connector/signing credentials are protected installation secrets. Auth stores and fills app sign-in credentials for password-based apps (AUTH-SWA-001); it is not a personal password manager or a general secrets-management product for infrastructure secrets and API keys, which stay with 1Password, AWS Secrets Manager or HashiCorp Vault. It supplies identity, app-role assignments, scoped API credentials and permission decisions; app owners enforce business-resource rules and retain ownership of app data. No group/claim implies authority until its target enforces it.
- **Compliance and operations:** [Evidence](https://github.com/just-every/run-evidence) owns audit readiness and auditor workspaces. Auth supplies exportable control evidence without claiming SOC 2 certification. [Uptime](https://github.com/just-every/run-uptime) owns external monitoring and [Oncall](https://github.com/just-every/run-oncall) paging; Datadog/Splunk/Sentinel own log analytics. Auth still owns visible durable outcomes and operation of its identity service.
- **Delivery and external custody:** Amazon SES/Base mail and Twilio deliver messages; licensed SaaS providers enforce remote account/session controls. Provider acceptance is not human receipt, completed access or verified revocation. Where a vendor cannot revoke a session, the employer sees the residual risk, expiry and manual/partner step. No universal compatibility, uptime promise, region guarantee, identity-document verification or telecom network is claimed without the declared integration and measurement.
- **Portability and issuer limits:** Base command and transport machinery is retained, while Auth replaces Base operator privilege with its own proof, private authorization, identity archive and grant-map policy. AUTH-TRANSFER-001 records the current narrower replacement; AUTH-TRANSFER-002, AUTH-ERASURE-001, AUTH-RETENTION-001 and AUTH-AUDIT-001 define the complete target. Copied Base erasure/portability source alone is not product acceptance. Host moves require reenrollment/re-linking; an archive cannot reproduce hardware private keys or reveal a later erasure journal it was never given.
