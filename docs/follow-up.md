# Follow-up

Activity records unresolved enrollment, recovery, provisioning, app setup and delegated assignment work independently of reminder delivery. The source action decides completion, cancellation or expiry. A sent reminder never proves that access was granted or removed.

App setup completes only after the actual link callback confirms it. A human-approved proposal remains visible if its callback fails; revoking a preparer key does not end a proposal already entrusted to people. Delegated assignment follow-up tracks employee consent, independent approval and exact execution, and stops with the task’s authority. It never claims downstream access from local assignment completion.

The current slice sends escalation email only to explicitly approved, currently active organization owners and administrators. It does not yet send employee, decider or help-desk reminders. Slack, Teams, Oncall, chat decisions, generic access requests and reviews remain unfinished under AUTH-FOLLOWUP-002/003/004.

An owner or administrator signs in with a hardware passkey and confirms their email in the same browser within ten minutes before enabling email, selecting recipients, or changing schedules. Without saved consent, the work is visible and nothing is sent. Recipients are organization person IDs selected from current administrators; arbitrary addresses and people outside the organization are refused. Every newly saved enabled schedule requires an approved escalation recipient, including when reminders stop at the deadline. Legacy policies with no escalation recipients are upgraded atomically on first use, including in an isolated organization. Only the administrator person IDs already approved for reminders are copied to escalation; no person or address is added. The upgrade is audited and reopens a legacy deadline-stopped reminder for its escalation. Each copied recipient must still be an active owner or administrator when dispatched. If none of the previously approved recipients remains eligible, no new recipient is invented: the failed recheck and redacted delivery error remain visible until the owner saves an eligible approved recipient. Current authority is checked before each dispatch. If a selected recipient loses their role or is suspended, they receive nothing; Auth records the failed recheck and immediately tries the policy’s approved owner/admin escalation recipients. If those recipients are also unavailable, the unresolved work and redacted delivery error remain visible.

Choose the employer timezone, business weekdays, holiday dates, local reminder time, business-day delay and deadline. Each obligation retains its calendar; explicitly rescheduling applies the current calendar to that item. A repeated daylight-saving minute uses its first occurrence; a skipped minute advances to the first real minute after the gap. Escalation repeats until the source resolves or stops reminders at the deadline, according to the saved policy. Stopping reminders leaves the unresolved work visible.

The recorded history distinguishes dispatch begun, transport accepted, delivery failure and source completion. History survives restart and is available through cursor pages at `GET /api/auth/followup`; `historyCursor` and `obligationCursor` retrieve earlier records. An interrupted outbound call remains recorded as dispatching; Auth does not claim delivery or access success from an uncertain result. A durable attempt is stored before transport for each recipient and scheduled occurrence, so a dispatch interrupted before its schedule advances is not sent again after its claim lease. This can leave delivery unknown even if transport never received the message; the next scheduled reminder is a separate attempt and never completes the source action.

AUTH-FOLLOWUP-002 additionally requires a published manage-follow-up permission, role-derived employee/decider/help-desk recipients with owner/admin escalation, and an authorized holder's early-send action. These are not offered by this slice. Existing sign-in and source-action proof still applies to every link in a reminder. Retention and authorized erasure govern stored follow-up data.

An unverified downstream provisioning or revocation raises a separate escalation at fifteen minutes after its event, to the policy-approved escalation recipients. This security escalation is independent of business hours, holidays and the ordinary reminder deadline. It is recorded once durably across restart and concurrent dispatch; failed or uncertain delivery stays visible. No policy means no outbound escalation. Verified or superseded work stops it, and an alert never verifies remote removal of access.
